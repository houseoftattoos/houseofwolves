About Mark

Hey, I’m Mark. In 2009 I first started tattooing on myself while living overseas and unable to work legally, which made a traditional apprenticeship impossible.

With a background in design, a lifelong habit of drawing, and a natural tendency to be an outlier and pirate, tattooing seemed like the perfect fit.

My personal and professional desire is to keep improving and moving forward in everything I do.
But I consider my greatest professional achievement to be having people trust me enough to let me tattoo their skin.
I enjoy being in nature, meditation, electronic music, various forms of kung fu and learning new things.

By Mark Noel

Geometric Tattooing

What geometric tattooing is

Geometric tattooing is built from structure: circles, triangles, lines, grids, dots and the patterns that grow out of them. Instead of picturing a subject, it works with balance, symmetry and repetition, and lets the design itself be the point. It can be a single mandala, a band of fine linework, a sleeve of interlocking shapes or a quiet pattern that follows the curve of one muscle.

A very old language

None of this is new. Geometry has decorated bodies, temples and objects for thousands of years. Islamic girih patterns tile a surface with stars and interlaced polygons, mandalas and the Sri Yantra come from Hindu and Buddhist traditions, and sacred geometry runs through cathedrals, textiles and tribal art across the world. I draw on that long history for its rhythm and proportion, not to copy any one tradition, but because those shapes have always felt right on a person.

Why it shouldn’t look like a machine made it

Geometric work has a reputation for being cold: perfect copies, ruler-straight, almost digital. I want the opposite. My designs are drawn by hand, and I let that show. Lines have a slight life to them, spacing breathes, and the pattern is composed around the person wearing it instead of stamped onto them. Symmetry gives the piece order. The hand gives it soul. You should be able to tell that a person made it, and that it was made for you.

Designed for the body, not the page

A body is not a flat sheet of paper. Shoulders turn, spines curve and forearms taper, so a pattern that looks perfect on paper can look wrong once it wraps around a limb. I design every piece to your shape and placement, so a mandala sits centred on the upper back, a sleeve flows from shoulder to wrist, and the lines follow the muscle and the movement. The result should look like it grew there.

Dotwork, linework and fine detail

Most of my geometric work is black, and the texture comes from how it is built. Dotwork creates soft, graduated shading out of thousands of tiny points. Fine, even linework keeps the structure crisp. Together they give depth without heavy shading, and they tend to age well when the line weight and spacing are planned properly. Where it suits the design, I leave open skin as part of the pattern so the piece has room to breathe.

Abstract and ornamental work

Abstract tattooing lets go of the need to depict anything. A sweep of lines across a shoulder, overlapping planes, a burst of angles or a loose cluster of shapes can carry movement and mood without ever becoming “a picture of” something. It suits people who want a tattoo that feels like theirs without a literal meaning attached. Ornamental work sits close to it: lace, filigree, jewellery-inspired panels and flowing patterns that decorate the body the way fine jewellery does, with symmetry and detail that follow the shape of the limb.

Nature in the lines

The natural world is where I look for balance. Petals, leaf veins, feathers, shells and the way a branch divides are all quietly geometric, and they appear in my work as botanical pieces and fine illustrative designs beside the pure pattern. If you want something that mixes the two, such as a skull among flowers, a shark inside a circle or a sleeve of linework and leaves, we can combine them in a way that holds together.

Working together on a custom design

I come to tattooing with a background in design and a lifelong habit of drawing, so I enjoy the planning as much as the tattooing. Every design is original and custom, drawn after we’ve talked through your ideas, placement and size. Nothing is copied from the internet or recycled from another client. I’ll bring the composition and you bring the meaning, or the lack of it, and we’ll refine it until it feels right.
