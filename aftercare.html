Aftercare

General advice for looking after a new tattoo while it heals. Your artist will go through aftercare with you at the end of your appointment, and what they tell you for your piece comes first.

Not medical advice

We're tattooists, not doctors. Everything on this page is general guidance only, and every person and every tattoo heals a little differently. If you're unwell, you have a health condition or an allergy that could affect healing, or you're worried about how your tattoo looks, speak to your doctor or pharmacist.

01

The first few hours

Your artist will cover the tattoo before you leave. Leave that covering on for as long as they told you to. Different coverings work differently. A clear adhesive film can stay on for days, while a bandage or plastic wrap usually comes off after a few hours. If you're not sure which one you've got, check with your artist.

When it does come off, don't be alarmed by what's underneath. It will look red and shiny, and it can have a thin layer of clear or ink-tinted fluid on it. That's normal.

02

Washing

  • Wash your hands first, every time you touch the tattoo.
  • Use lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free soap, and wash gently with your fingertips. No washcloths, sponges or loofahs.
  • Rinse until all the soap is gone, then pat it dry with a clean paper towel and let it air dry for a few minutes. A used towel can carry bacteria and can catch on flaking skin.
  • Wash it a couple of times a day for the first week or so, and after anything that makes it sweaty or dirty.
03

Moisturising

Once the tattoo is fully dry, put on a very thin layer of fragrance-free moisturiser or the aftercare product your artist recommended. It should leave the skin looking slightly soft, not wet or greasy. A thick layer keeps the skin wet and can slow healing, and a tattoo needs to breathe.

Some artists suggest leaving it dry for the first day or two before moisturising. If your artist told you something different from what's here, do what your artist said.

04

What healing looks like

Days 1 to 3
Red, tender and a little swollen. Some clear or ink-tinted fluid is normal.
Days 4 to 7
The skin feels tight and starts to itch. Light flaking and peeling begins.
Weeks 1 to 2
Flaking carries on and the tattoo can look dull or cloudy as new skin forms over it.
Weeks 2 to 4
The surface is usually healed, but it can still look a bit dull and feel dry.
Up to 2 or 3 months
The deeper layers of skin finish settling and the tattoo shows its real colour and contrast.

These times are a rough guide. Healing speed depends on the person, the size of the tattoo and where it is on the body.

05

What to avoid

  • Picking, peeling or scratching. It can pull ink out and leave scarring. If it itches, pat it lightly or moisturise.
  • Soaking. No baths, pools, spas, saunas or the ocean until it's fully healed, which is usually a few weeks. A quick shower is fine.
  • Direct sun and solariums. Keep it covered or in the shade while it heals.
  • Tight or rough clothing. Wear something loose and clean over it. Friction irritates healing skin.
  • Heavy sweating and gym work on or near the tattoo for the first few days, especially on areas that stretch or rub.
  • Shaving over it until the skin has healed.
  • Anything you haven't been told to use, such as scented lotions, oils or other creams.
  • Pets and dirty surfaces. Keep animals off it and keep it away from anything unclean.
06

Sleeping

Sleep on clean sheets in loose clothing, and try not to lie on the tattoo if you can help it. It might weep a little overnight in the first couple of days, so don't be surprised if the sheets pick up a few marks.

07

Once it's healed

  • Use sunscreen on it whenever it's exposed to the sun, SPF 50+ if you can. Sun is the main thing that fades a tattoo over the years.
  • Keep the skin moisturised. Healthy skin holds a tattoo better.
  • If a spot has healed patchy or lighter than you expected, message your artist on Instagram once it's fully healed so they can have a look. Touch-ups are normal, especially on fine line work and on places that rub, like fingers and feet.
08

Seeing a doctor

Some redness, swelling and tenderness in the first few days is normal. See a doctor if you notice any of these, and let your artist know as well:

  • Redness that spreads beyond the tattoo or keeps getting worse after the first few days.
  • Swelling, heat or pain that is increasing instead of easing.
  • Yellow or green pus, or a bad smell coming from it.
  • Red streaks running away from the tattoo.
  • Fever, chills or feeling generally unwell.
  • A rash, hives or intense itching, which can be an allergic reaction.
  • Lumps or raised areas that appear later on.

If you have a serious reaction, call 000.
