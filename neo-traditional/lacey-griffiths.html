By Lacey Griffiths

Neo-Traditional

What neo-traditional is

Neo-traditional starts from the strong foundations of old school tattooing: a clear outline, a bold shape and a design that reads from across the room. Then it opens everything up. The palette is far wider, the shading is softer, and the subjects can be anything from a fairy to a fox in a kimono. If American Traditional is a rule book, neo-traditional is what happens when you keep the rules and let the imagination loose.

Where the softness comes from

The style grew out of American Traditional and picked up a lot from illustration along the way. Art Nouveau, the flowing, plant-filled poster art of the late 1800s and early 1900s, is a big part of its look: sweeping curves, ornate borders and flowers worked into frames and portraits. That's why so many neo-traditional pieces feel like a page from an old storybook. The lines can thicken and thin, the colour can melt from one shade into the next, and a tattoo can feel painted rather than stamped.

Flowers, moths and everything pretty

Nature is where I start. Peonies, chrysanthemums and cherry blossoms are some of my favourite things to draw, because they suit this style so well: layered petals, rich colour and curves that follow the body. Blush pinks, peach, coral and soft lilac are some of the prettiest colours to work with, and they sit beautifully next to deeper greens and teals. Add a luna moth, a butterfly or a bird and you have a piece that feels alive and delicate without losing its strength.

Fantasy, folklore and a bit of magic

Mythology is the other half of my work. Unicorns and winged horses, fairies, potion bottles, crystals and moonlit skies are all fair game, and so are stories from other cultures, like a kitsune in a flowing kimono or a female samurai wrapped in blossoms. I like to design these as small worlds, with a clear main character and enough flowers, stars and little details around it that you can keep finding something new every time you look.

Colour that lasts

Jewel tones are the signature of neo-traditional: deep teal, ruby, amethyst and gold, with blush and peach for the softer pieces. The trick is keeping the colour rich, because lighter pastel and pink shades need to be packed in properly to hold up. I build the colour in smooth, even layers, with solid dark outlines and shading underneath so the design keeps its structure as it settles and ages. The goal is a tattoo that still looks like the day you left the studio.

Quirky, cute and a little bit odd

Not everything has to be serious. I love anything quirky: a fairy riding a mouse, a fruit bat with a pomegranate, a portrait of your cat among peonies. Neo-traditional has room for humour and personality, and the best custom pieces are the ones that could only belong to you. If it makes you smile, it probably belongs on the wall of ideas.

Illustrious illustrations

My illustrative side is where the line work gets its character. Illustrative tattoos take their cues from book art, botanical drawings and fine engravings, with lighter, more detailed lines and delicate shading, sometimes in colour and sometimes in black and grey. I'm just as happy working in this style as in bold neo-traditional, and I've also picked up geometric and traditional work along the way after years in busy street shops, so I can match the look to the idea instead of the other way around.

Comfort first, technique always

I've been tattooing since 2013. I started in Geelong, then spent nine years working in Melbourne before settling back in Geelong at House of Wolves. I enjoy custom work at every scale, from a small piece that fits on the inside of your wrist to a full back piece, and I treat both the same way: make the tattoo as technically perfect as I can, while making sure the experience is as comfortable and enjoyable as possible. Many people are nervous before their first appointment, and that's completely fine. We'll go at a pace that works for you.
